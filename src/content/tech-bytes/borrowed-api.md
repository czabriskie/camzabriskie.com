---
title: "Everything Spotify let me have"
description: "Building a song-guessing party game on Spotify's API meant living with a running list of decisions Spotify made that I never got a vote on."
date: 2026-09-07T10:00:00Z
---

The other game I built plays like Heads Up but with songs instead of words. You hold the phone on your forehead in landscape, a song from one of your own Spotify playlists plays out loud, everyone else can see the title and artist on the screen, and you guess before the timer runs out. Tilt the phone down for correct, up for pass. It works, and it's genuinely fun with a group, and none of that is really what I want to write about. I think more now about the list of things I don't control, because I built this thing on top of somebody else's API, and every one of those things is a decision Spotify made for reasons that are theirs, not mine.

Start with how you even get in. Signing in uses something called PKCE OAuth, which is a way of letting someone log in through Spotify without your app having to hold onto a secret key of its own, which matters because a secret sitting in an app that anyone can install is not really a secret. I picked Spotify's plain Web API over their App Remote SDK on purpose, mostly because the App Remote SDK ships as a binary that isn't on Maven Central, which is where Android projects normally pull their dependencies from, and I didn't want that sitting in the repo, plus the sign-in stays simpler with the Web API. That choice is exactly where the annoying parts come from. Playback happens through the Spotify app already running on the phone, treated as a device on what Spotify calls Connect, and if Spotify hasn't played anything recently it just doesn't show up as an available device at all, so a round can't start until someone opens Spotify and taps a song for a second first. And it requires Spotify Premium, full stop, because playback control is a Premium feature.

Some of the rest is just the ground shifting under a working app. The endpoint I was using to read a playlist's songs, the old `/tracks` path, started returning a 403 error one day, which is the standard "you're not allowed to do that" response, and the fix was just moving to a different path, `/items`, that returns basically the same data. Fine, that's the kind of thing you expect from any API over time. Less expected: Spotify only lets an app read the contents of playlists you own or collaborate on. Any playlist you just follow, including Spotify's own editorial playlists, the "Rap Caviar" type stuff, is completely invisible to the app. I can't read what's in it. So the playlist picker just hides those and tells you how many it skipped, and if you want to play one you have to go copy its songs into a playlist of your own first.

The chorus feature stung a little, because it's genuinely the nicest thing in the game. Instead of starting every song at the intro, which is usually the part nobody recognizes, it jumps to the most identifiable part of the track. That was built on Spotify's audio analysis endpoint, which looks at a song and returns its sections, and the app just picks the loudest section in roughly the middle third of the track and starts there. Except Spotify stopped serving that endpoint to apps registered after November 2024, and my app is a fresh client ID, so it falls back to a heuristic instead: start 30% into the song, never later than 30 seconds before the end, and if the song is a minute or less just play it from the top. It's a fine fallback, it mostly works, but it's a guess standing in for something that used to be a real answer, because the real answer got turned off before I ever had a chance to use it.

And then there's the stuff around the edges of all this. Spotify now requires a Premium account just to register a developer app in the first place, not just to use one, and they've paused new app creation entirely for stretches of time, so depending on when you show up, the "create app" button on their dashboard might just not be there.

None of this is Spotify wronging me. A company narrowing what a third-party app can do, tightening who gets served audio analysis, deciding Premium is the price of entry for developers too, all of that probably makes sense from inside Spotify for reasons I don't get to see. It's their API, their playlist data, their business. I'm not owed any of it.

But there's a switch on the setup screen, "play songs through Spotify," and it was built as a nice option, a way to still play the game if you wanted to keep it quiet or didn't feel like dealing with playback that day. Turn it off and the game just shows the title and artist and everyone hums or describes the song instead, and no call to Spotify happens during the round at all, none of the device stuff, none of the chorus stuff, nothing. It was the feature I almost didn't bother with. And nobody can take it away from me, because it doesn't ask Spotify for anything. Everything else in this game exists at the pleasure of decisions I don't get a vote on. That one doesn't.

I don't think it's an accident that the newer game I built, the rhythm one, makes no network calls at all. I didn't sit down and decide that as a lesson learned, but I don't think it's a coincidence either. The parts of a project you actually own, the ones that don't depend on someone else's endpoint still being there next year, are kind of the only parts you can really promise anyone are going to keep working.
