---
title: I just built the game myself
description: Tired of ad-locked kids' puzzle games, I spent a night building one instead, and I've kept adding to it ever since.
date: 2026-09-07
---

I've gotten so sick of games that auto install themselves on my phone, and then once you actually open them it's video ads every couple minutes, or the whole thing is quietly built so you can't really win without spending money or a chunk of your time unlocking something. My kid likes puzzle games, so I went looking for a few she'd enjoy, and no matter what I tried it was the same story. Even when she did everything right, played a level perfectly, there was still no path to actually winning it without watching a video or paying for something. That one got me more than the ads did, honestly, the idea that being good at the game wasn't enough.

At some point I just stopped looking and thought, with Claude, I could probably build this myself faster than I could keep shopping around for something that wasn't secretly designed against her. So I did. I'm not going to pretend that's some kind of feat, it's just that once I framed it that way, building suddenly felt like the easier option than searching, which says more about the state of what's out there than it does about me.

I got a playable version done in one night, and I mean that plainly, not as a highlight. It was rough, and I've been fixing bugs and adding features pretty much every time since then that I've sat and watched her play, but by the time she woke up the next morning there was something there she could actually pick up and play through.

It ended up being a rhythm game, a bee hopping flower to flower on the beat of a song while you tap along, and I picked that because she's been in a music class the last few weeks working on keeping a beat, so I figured I could make something that helped with that and was fun on its own, songs she already knows instead of some made-up thing.

The part I keep coming back to is what I actually get out of doing it this way that I couldn't have bought. I know exactly what's in the thing she's playing, every song, every rule, because I wrote all of it. And I can point it at whatever she's actually working on at any given moment, right now that's rhythm, later it'll be something else, in a way no app store search is ever going to hand me.

For what it's worth on the other side of the thing I was originally annoyed about: there's no ads in it, no video to sit through, it doesn't even talk to a network at all. There is a small one-time payment planned for the later levels eventually, and even that comes with a rule I set for myself, that money never buys progress, a level you pay for still has to be earned the same way as any other. It's not built yet and it's just one payment instead of a whole system designed to wear you down, which was kind of the point of doing this in the first place.

There's more to say about how it actually got built, the mechanic and the tools and all of that, but that's a different post. This part was just about why I bothered at all.
