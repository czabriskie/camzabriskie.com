My kid likes puzzle games, so I went looking for a few she'd enjoy, and every one I tried was the same shape underneath. Video ads every couple of minutes, or a level she could play perfectly and still not clear without watching something or paying for something. The ads annoyed me. Being good at the game not being enough annoyed me more, and it's a strange lesson to hand a kid through a toy.

So I stopped looking and built one instead, with Claude doing most of the typing, and had something playable by the time she woke up the next morning. I want to be plain about that rather than make it sound impressive, it was rough and I've been fixing bugs and adding things every time I sit and watch her play since. And building had quietly become the easier option than searching, which says more about what's in the store than it does about me.

What I got out of it is two things I couldn't have bought. I know exactly what's in the thing she's playing, every song and every rule, because I wrote all of them. And I can aim it at whatever she's actually working on right now, which at the moment is keeping a beat in a music class, so the game is a bee hopping flower to flower on the beat of songs she already knows.

The shift isn't that AI lets you ship faster. It's that the threshold for "worth building yourself" dropped low enough that a small, specific, unglamorous thing for exactly one person is now on the table, and a lot of what we settle for from app stores is only there because that threshold used to be high.
