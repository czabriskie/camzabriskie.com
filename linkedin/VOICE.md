# LinkedIn voice — style layer

Snapshot of the Postbeam account training's **Requirements** and **Mission**
(taken 2026-08-27, when the account was retired — Decision 0005), kept because
the weekly drafting routine reads this file as its "Layer 1" style source. The
training's Content Sources section is deliberately NOT carried over: topics come
only from Cam's own Readwise notes and vault, never from searches or subreddits.
The training's original Audience section is replaced by the current one below.

The routine's own prompt carries "Layer 2" (the hard voice rules: run-on
sentences, no em dashes, no hashtags, plain vocabulary, and so on). Layer 2 wins
wherever the two disagree.

## Style requirements

- Opens with a concept or source, then reframes it through personal experience.
  (For a journal-sourced post there is no article: the entry point is the
  concrete thing Cam actually did or built. Never invent a source.)
- Uses casual asides mid-paragraph like "the way I think about it" or "the
  thing is".
- Paragraphs run 3–5 sentences, building one idea per block.
- No bullet points or numbered lists, always flowing prose.
- Closes by circling back to the broader principle behind the personal anecdote.
- Tone is conversational and reflective, like thinking out loud to a peer.
- Uses concrete technical examples to ground abstract concepts.

## Mission

Cameron Zabriskie writes about the intersection of software reliability,
AI-assisted development, and the practical realities of building and integrating
software systems. Posts draw on engineering reading (like the SRE book) and
hands-on experience, exploring how individual developers can apply
infrastructure and platform-engineering thinking to their own workflows,
especially as AI tooling changes what solo builders can accomplish.

## Audience (updated 2026-08-22)

Primary: people learning software engineering — students, early-career
engineers, career switchers, job seekers. Secondary: the experienced-engineer
peers who followed the earlier material. Explain insider terms in half a
sentence rather than assuming them, and make the "so what" concrete enough that
someone five years behind Cam can act on it. Do not dumb anything down and do
not change the voice.
